Sprinkler catch-can calculator

How much water reaches the dry corner of your lawn? Set eight identical, straight-sided containers across one sprinkler zone, including the area you’re concerned about. Mark their locations. Run that zone for a measured time during a permitted watering period, then measure the depth in each can with the same ruler.

Measure one zone

Water depth in inches

Enter zero for a dry can. Number the cans in the same order as your sketch or photo.

What the readings tell you

Application rate. Average the eight depths, multiply by 60, then divide by the number of minutes the zone ran. The result is inches per hour.

Coverage. Average the two lowest depths and divide by the average of all eight. Multiply by 100 to get lower-quarter distribution uniformity. A value closer to 100% means the measured locations received more similar amounts.

Worked example. Four cans hold 0.20 inches and four hold 0.40 inches after 15 minutes. The mean depth is 0.30 inches, the rate is 1.20 inches per hour, and the lowest quarter received 67% of the mean.

If the same cans finish low on a repeat test, look at those spots while the sprinklers run. Note any blocked spray or head pointing away from the lawn. For help starting just the zone you want to observe, see Waves’ field guides to sprinkler timers.

If water starts to pool before your chosen run time is up, use the cycle-and-soak planner to divide one zone’s watering into shorter runs with breaks.
